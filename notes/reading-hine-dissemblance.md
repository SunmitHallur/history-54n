# Reading notes · Hine (1989), culture of dissemblance

- Core claim to track in the essay: dissemblance as collective practice of concealment under sexualized surveillance, not reducible to shame or silence-as-absence.
- Compare with Higginbotham: where respectability is public leverage, dissemblance guards interior life the archive rarely documents fairly.
- Questions for class: how does Hine’s Middle West frame transfer to visual sources (cartes de visite, portraits) that pretend transparency?
